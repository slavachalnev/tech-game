# Cornwall, 1705: The Drowned Mine

*Player-facing briefing. Safe to read.*

**Arrival.** Monday 2 April 1705, Redruth, in the tin and copper country of west Cornwall. Queen Anne is on the throne, and England is at war with France. Newcomen's first engine is seven years away.

**Who you are.** A stranger of modest means, lately "up from the country", with no family here, no trade and no local standing. You can read, write and reckon. Everything you know from the 21st century is in your head, and only that. Your hands belong to a modern person: you have no practised period craft.

**What you have.**
- A purse of £50. A skilled smith earns about 9s a week, and a labourer about 6s.
- A small rented smithy on the edge of Redruth, with hearth, bellows, anvil and tools. The rent is paid up to Midsummer.
- Jacca Pascoe, a local smith, on wages.
- Bar iron and coal for ordinary work, plus drawing instruments.
- A letter of introduction to Nicholas Penrose, purser of Wheal Fortune.

**The situation.** Wheal Fortune, two miles east of town, is drowning. Horse whims draining the mine day and night only just hold the water at 24 fathoms, and the richest ground lies beneath it. The adventurers (shareholders) have posted an offer: **£250 for any means that holds the water below 30 fathoms for a month, and £60 a year for as long as it keeps working.** Others have tried and failed.

**How to play.** Say what you do, in as much detail as you actually know. The referee credits only what you state or sketch: naming a thing isn't knowing how to make it. Every action takes time and most cost money. Start a message with `ooc:` to ask the referee a question out of character.

**Settings.** Strict referee. Starting wealth £50. No time limit beyond one lifetime. There is no win condition; progress is measured.
